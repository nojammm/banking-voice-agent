Westpac says it is committed to upholding the Australian banking industry's standards of practice and service set out in the Banking Code of Practice. The current version of the Code took effect on 28 February 2025 and replaced the 2019 Code, which still applies to certain agreements made before that date. The Code covers individual and small business customers and their individual guarantors. Where the Code applies, Westpac describes it as a legally binding agreement between Westpac and its customers.

Westpac says money in a bank account is considered "unclaimed" when the account has a balance of $500 or more and there have been no customer deposits or withdrawals for 7 years or longer. Interest earned and fees charged do not count as activity. Westpac must close these accounts and send the funds to ASIC by 31 March each year. To reclaim the money, search ASIC's Moneysmart unclaimed money tool, note the Original Transaction Number, and take the completed claim form to a Westpac branch with two forms of ID (one with a photo) and proof of account ownership. Westpac says the process can take up to 3 months.

Under Westpac's Personal Accounts Terms and Conditions (effective 27 August 2026), a personal deposit account becomes inactive if there are no deposits or withdrawals for a continuous six months and the balance is nil, or in debit without an arrangement; Westpac may close an inactive account without notice. Westpac may also close an account after giving reasonable notice, for example for unsatisfactory conduct, business use of a personal account, or where it reasonably believes the account's use has caused harm, such as financial abuse, to another person. In some cases it may act without prior notice, but Westpac says it will act fairly and reasonably.

The Financial Claims Scheme (FCS) is an Australian Government scheme that, if activated after a bank fails, protects certain deposits up to $250,000 per account holder per eligible authorised deposit-taking institution (ADI). APRA administers the FCS and aims to pay most customers within seven calendar days. Westpac, St.George, Bank of Melbourne and BankSA all operate under one Westpac banking licence, so a customer's combined deposits across those brands, plus eligible Westpac products sold under third-party brands, share one $250,000 limit.

Westpac's Personal Accounts Terms and Conditions (effective 27 August 2026) say Westpac will always let customers know if the terms and conditions of their account change. Westpac gives at least 30 days' notice of a new or increased fee and of changes it believes are unfavourable to the customer. Advance notice may not be given where a change is needed immediately to restore or maintain security, or to manage a material and immediate risk. Interest rate changes are notified no later than the day of the change, except where the rate is linked to money market or other rates. Notice may be in writing, electronically or by media advertisement.
