Westpac customers affected by domestic or family violence or financial abuse can contact Westpac's specialist Priority Assist team on 1800 063 509. Anyone in immediate danger should call 000. Help Westpac says it may provide includes: financial hardship support; changing joint accounts to "all to sign"; reviewing third-party authorities; cancelling redraw facilities or additional cards where safe; helping separate finances; updating contact details, resetting online banking passwords and changing PINs; opening an account that only the customer can access; adjusting limits; gambling blocks; and reporting abusive payment messages in the Westpac App.

Westpac customers who are deaf, hard of hearing or have a speech or communication difficulty can contact Westpac through the National Relay Service (NRS). TTY users phone 133 677 then ask for 132 032. Voice Relay users phone 1300 555 727 then ask for 132 032. NRS Chat users connect to the NRS and then ask for 132 032. Westpac's Personal Accounts Terms and Conditions also say customers can message Westpac within the Westpac App.

To tell Westpac that a customer has died, contact the Westpac Estates Management team on 1300 130 240 (Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm AEST), +61 2 9155 7590 from overseas, or estatesmanagement@westpac.com.au. Questions about a deceased person's home loan go to 132 558. Westpac's support guide (April 2026) asks for a completed Deceased Customer Notification & Estate Form and one document confirming the death, such as a death certificate, medical or police report, or probate or letters of administration. Send certified copies, not originals. Within 14 business days Westpac sends correspondence confirming what it has done and the next steps.

-->
Westpac says anyone can let Westpac know when someone has died, including through deathnotification.gov.au if the death was registered in Australia. Only the next of kin, the executor or a legal representative of the estate can manage the estate. After notification, sole accounts in the deceased's name are restrained, which stops direct debits and recurring payments, although deposits can still be received. Loan repayments must continue and interest will still accrue. Joint account holders keep access to joint accounts as usual.

Westpac's deceased estates support guide (April 2026) says a Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration is required if the estate balance with Westpac is $100,000 or more, if the estate includes secured lending, or if an "Estate of the Late" account is wanted (that account can only be set up in a branch). Simpler documents apply below $100,000 with no secured lending. Westpac can pay the funeral director directly from the estate once it has the funeral invoice. Once Westpac has all required documents, it completes estate instructions within 14 business days.

Westpac recognises three ways a trusted person can help with banking. A power of attorney (general or enduring) is set up through an in-branch process, with legal paperwork and an appointment required; an enduring power of attorney must be set up while the person still has capacity to make decisions. An account signatory can operate the account, set up in a branch. An authorised third party, such as a financial counsellor, can speak with Westpac by phone but does not have direct access to the customer's accounts.
