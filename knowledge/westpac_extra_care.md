Westpac customers affected by domestic or family violence or financial abuse can contact Westpac's specialist Priority Assist team on 1800 063 509. Anyone in immediate danger should call 000. Westpac says customers can talk to its team securely and confidentially, and that their safety and privacy are its priority. For 24/7 counselling, Westpac points to 1800RESPECT on 1800 737 732. Help Westpac says it may provide includes: financial hardship support; changing joint accounts to "all to sign"; reviewing third-party authorities; cancelling redraw facilities or additional cards where safe; helping separate finances; updating contact details, resetting online banking passwords and changing PINs; opening an account that only the customer can access; adjusting limits; gambling blocks; and reporting abusive payment messages in the Westpac App.

Westpac says anyone can let Westpac know when someone has died, including through deathnotification.gov.au if the death was registered in Australia. Only the next of kin, the executor or a legal representative of the estate can manage the estate. After notification, sole accounts in the deceased's name are restrained, which stops direct debits and recurring payments, although deposits can still be received. Loan repayments must continue and interest will still accrue. Joint account holders keep access to joint accounts as usual.

Westpac's deceased estates support guide (April 2026) says a Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration is required if the estate balance with Westpac is $100,000 or more, if the estate includes secured lending, or if an "Estate of the Late" account is wanted (that account can only be set up in a branch). Simpler documents apply below $100,000 with no secured lending. Westpac can pay the funeral director directly from the estate once it has the funeral invoice. Once Westpac has all required documents, it completes estate instructions within 14 business days.

Westpac's deceased estates page says that when the primary cardholder dies, Westpac cancels secondary cards and any debt is offset from available accounts, where permitted; a secondary cardholder can apply for a new card, subject to approval. Home loan and personal loan repayments must continue, by joint holders or the estate, and interest will accrue. Westpac describes the Executor as the person named in the Will to manage assets, pay debts and distribute the estate, and says that if someone dies without a Will, debts are paid first and then the remaining funds are distributed. Powers of attorney are no longer valid after death. Anyone struggling with a Westpac loan or card after the death of a partner or carer can call Westpac Assist on 1800 067 497.

## Power of attorney and other ways a trusted person can help with Westpac banking

-->
Westpac lists seven ways a trusted person can help with banking: an authorised third party, an account signatory, account sharing, third-party access, a general power of attorney, an enduring power of attorney, and a financial management order. A general or enduring power of attorney is a legal document, and Westpac's set-up is an in-branch process with legal paperwork and an appointment required; Westpac suggests considering independent legal advice first. A general power of attorney ends if the person can no longer make decisions. An enduring power of attorney must be set up while the person still has capacity to make decisions, and continues after they lose capacity. A financial management order is made by a court or tribunal, usually when the person can no longer make decisions and has no enduring power of attorney.

## Westpac signatories, account sharing, third-party access and authorised third parties

-->
Westpac describes four ways to give a trusted person access without a power of attorney. An authorised third party can speak with Westpac on the customer's behalf, for example to request hardship help or make a complaint, but cannot access accounts or make transactions; set up by posting or emailing an Authorised third-party form. An account signatory can manage the account in branch only (in-branch access only); add one with a Notice of authority form in branch, or through Online Banking or the Westpac App. Account sharing lets a trusted person access the account in Online Banking with a separate profile, with view-only or full access, set up in Online Banking. Third-party access lets another Westpac customer see the account alongside their own, set up with a form in branch.

Westpac says that if a customer is concerned the person they appointed as their authority is now causing them harm, they can contact Westpac's Priority Assist team on 1800 063 509, Monday to Friday 8.30am to 7.30pm Sydney time. If someone with access to the account is spending money in a way that doesn't benefit the customer, or there are unusual transactions or unauthorised changes, Westpac says to report it immediately.
