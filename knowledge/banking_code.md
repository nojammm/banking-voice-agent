The Banking Code of Practice is the Australian banking industry's code, published by the Australian Banking Association (ABA). The 2025 Banking Code of Practice was approved by ASIC on 27 June 2024 and is effective from 28 February 2025. The independent Banking Code Compliance Committee monitors banks' compliance with the Code. The Code replaces the version dated 5 October 2021. A 2026 Banking Code Review is underway.

Under the Banking Code of Practice (effective 28 February 2025), Financial Difficulty means being unable to repay what you owe, expecting to be unable to pay upcoming repayments, or having difficulty meeting repayment obligations. Examples of help include short-term interest-only payments, extending the loan term to reduce repayments, or temporarily postponing or deferring payments, which may increase total interest. The bank will tell the customer in writing whether it will help and the reasons for its decision. Customers can ask the bank to deal with their financial counsellor or representative. The bank will not require a customer to access superannuation to repay a loan (unless borrowing for a self-managed super fund), and will not sell the debt while actively considering the customer's financial situation.

Under the Banking Code of Practice (effective 28 February 2025), before accepting a guarantee the bank will take reasonable steps to hold a meeting with the prospective guarantor, in person or by video, phone or other means, without the borrower present, unless the guarantor has received independent legal advice or another exception applies. The bank will not accept a guarantee until the third day after the guarantor has been given the required information, unless an exception applies. Within 14 days of the relevant event, the bank will send the guarantor copies of formal demands or default notices, and notices about borrower hardship changes or a continuing default of more than two months.

Under the Banking Code of Practice (effective 28 February 2025), banks will take reasonable measures to make their services accessible, including for older customers, people with disability, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander customers and people with limited English. Where practicable, banks will organise or refer customers to free external support, including interpreter services (including Auslan) and the National Relay Service. Banks will help Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander customers meet identification requirements when they lack standard ID documents, following AUSTRAC's guidance. Customers who tell the bank they hold a Commonwealth Seniors Health Card, Health Care Card or Pensioner Concession Card will be given information about basic, low-fee or no-fee accounts.

Under the Banking Code of Practice (effective 28 February 2025), banks commit to taking extra care with customers who are experiencing vulnerability. Circumstances the Code lists include age, disability, mental health conditions, elder abuse, family and/or domestic violence, financial abuse, Financial Difficulty and language barriers. The Code encourages customers to tell their bank about their circumstances so the bank can provide appropriate support.

Under the Banking Code of Practice (effective 28 February 2025), once a bank has received the verified documents it needs, it will act on a valid Deceased Estate Representative instruction about a deceased's accounts within 14 business days. For accounts in the deceased's sole name, the bank will promptly identify and stop charging fees for banking services that can no longer be provided. Before probate, a representative can still request account information and give instructions to release funds to pay or reimburse permissible expenses such as funeral expenses.

Under the Banking Code of Practice (effective 28 February 2025), a business is a Small Business if it, together with any related entities, had an annual turnover of less than $10 million in the previous financial year, has fewer than 100 full-time equivalent employees, and has less than $5 million total debt to all credit providers. Small Business customers receive the Code's protections alongside individual customers.
