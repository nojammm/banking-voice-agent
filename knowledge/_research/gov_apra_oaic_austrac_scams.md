## 1. Financial Claims Scheme (APRA)

Note: fcs.gov.au returns a 302 redirect to https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme.

1. **Fact:** The FCS protects deposits up to $250,000 per account holder per authorised deposit-taking institution (ADI).
   - **Quote:** "The FCS is a government-backed safety net for ADI deposits of up to $250,000 per account holder per ADI."
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-explains-financial-claims-scheme
   - **Page date / version:** Published 20 July 2020 [FETCHED]

2. **Fact:** The FCS covers ADIs (banks, building societies, credit unions) that are incorporated in Australia and authorised by APRA. It does not cover branches of foreign banks in Australia.
   - **Quote:** "The FCS covers ADIs incorporated in Australia and authorised by APRA that are" banks, building societies or credit unions; deposit checker: FCS does not cover "branches of foreign banks in Australia."
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/about-financial-claims-scheme ; https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme/deposit-checker-are-your-deposits-protected
   - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026) [FETCHED]

3. **Fact:** The FCS can be activated only by the Australian Government, and only if an institution fails. APRA administers it once it is activated.
   - **Quote:** "The FCS can be activated by the Australian Government in the unlikely event that an ADI or general insurer fails."
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/about-financial-claims-scheme
   - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026) [FETCHED]

4. **Fact:** The $250,000 limit applies to all of a person's deposits held under one banking licence, even when the licence covers several brands. APRA's examples: St George is part of Westpac, and RAMS deposit accounts are Westpac accounts.
   - **Quote:** "For example, BankWest is part of the Commonwealth Bank, while St George is part of Westpac." / "deposit accounts offered by RAMS are actually Westpac accounts."
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme/banks-building-societies-and-credit-unions/different-banking-businesses
   - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026) [FETCHED]

5. **Fact:** A person can hold up to $250,000 at each of several different ADIs, and each holding is protected separately.
   - **Quote:** "So a person can have up to $250,000 in deposit accounts with a number of different ADIs"
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/frequently-asked-questions-about-banking-and-financial-claims-scheme
   - **Page date / version:** Published 1 September 2019 [FETCHED]

6. **Fact:** For a joint account, the balance is split equally between the account holders. Each holder's share is then added to their other deposits at the same ADI.
   - **Quote:** "For joint accounts, the amount of FCS protection is determined by splitting the deposit equally between the account holders."
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/frequently-asked-questions-about-banking-and-financial-claims-scheme
   - **Page date / version:** Published 1 September 2019 [FETCHED]

7. **Fact:** Covered account types include savings, call, term deposit, cheque, transaction, cash management and trustee accounts, and offset accounts that are separate deposit accounts.
   - **Quote:** "savings accounts", "call accounts", "term deposits", "transaction accounts", "cash management accounts", "mortgage offset accounts (either 100 per cent or partial offset) that are separate deposit accounts", "trustee accounts"
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/types-accounts-covered-under-financial-claims-scheme
   - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026) [FETCHED]

8. **Fact:** Not covered: foreign-currency accounts, accounts at overseas branches of Australian banks, credit balances on credit cards or loans, and prepaid cards.
   - **Quote:** "accounts with funds that are not in Australian dollars"; "accounts kept at overseas branches of Australian banks"; "credit balances on credit card facilities or other loans"; "pre-paid card facilities or similar products"
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/types-accounts-covered-under-financial-claims-scheme
   - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026) [FETCHED]

9. **Fact:** If the FCS is activated, APRA aims to pay most account holders within seven calendar days. None of the APRA pages says payment is fully automatic. They say APRA and the institution will contact depositors about any steps they need to take.
   - **Quote:** "will endeavour to make payments to the majority of account holders within seven calendar days"; APRA and the institution will tell depositors "any steps they need to take to access their FCS payment."
   - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/frequently-asked-questions-about-activation-payments-and-administration-of-financial-claims-scheme
   - **Page date / version:** Published 1 September 2019 [FETCHED]

10. **Fact:** Payment is usually made by cheque or electronically to another account nominated by the account holder, and only in Australian dollars. To recover amounts above $250,000, the depositor must make a claim in the liquidation.
    - **Quote:** "either by cheque or electronically to an alternate account nominated by the account holder"; an account holder seeking more than the limit must "make a claim" in the liquidation.
    - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/frequently-asked-questions-about-activation-payments-and-administration-of-financial-claims-scheme
    - **Page date / version:** Published 1 September 2019 [FETCHED]

11. **Fact:** Customers can check their coverage with APRA's Deposit Checker and its list of covered ADIs. If their institution is not listed, they should contact it.
    - **Quote:** "If you're not sure whether your account is covered, visit our Deposit Checker." / "You should contact your financial institution to check."
    - **URL:** https://www.apra.gov.au/frequently-asked-questions-about-coverage-of-financial-claims-scheme ; https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme/deposit-checker-are-your-deposits-protected
    - **Page date / version:** 1 September 2019 / not shown [FETCHED]

## 2. Privacy (OAIC)

12. **Fact:** There are 13 Australian Privacy Principles. APP 12 requires an organisation to give a person access to the personal information it holds about them unless a specific exception applies.
    - **Quote:** "This includes a requirement to provide access unless a specific exception applies."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-quick-reference
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

13. **Fact:** APP 13 covers correction. People can ask for information that is inaccurate, out of date, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading to be corrected. Correction is free.
    - **Quote:** "Australian privacy law gives you the right to correct the personal information"; "Is there a charge?" "No."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/your-personal-information/correct-your-personal-information
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

14. **Fact:** The OAIC considers 30 days a reasonable time for an organisation to respond to an access request or a correction request.
    - **Quote:** "We think 30 days is a reasonable period."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/your-personal-information/access-your-personal-information (and the correct-your-personal-information page)
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

15. **Fact:** An organisation may charge for giving access, but the charge must not be excessive or used to discourage requests. If it refuses access, it must give its reasons in writing and explain how to complain.
    - **Quote:** "An organisation can't use this charge to discourage you from requesting access to your personal information."; they must explain "their reasons for refusing and how you can make a complaint."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/your-personal-information/access-your-personal-information
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

16. **Fact:** If a correction is refused, the person can ask for a statement that the information is wrong to be attached to it. The organisation must take reasonable steps to do this.
    - **Quote:** The organisation "must take reasonable steps to associate the statement"
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/your-personal-information/correct-your-personal-information
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

17. **Fact:** A person must complain to the organisation first. If it does not respond within 30 days, or the response is unsatisfactory, they can complain to the OAIC. The complaint must be in writing.
    - **Quote:** "you first need to complain to the organisation or agency you think has mishandled it."; "If they don't respond to your complaint within 30 days"; "The Privacy Act 1988 requires that your complaint to us must be made in writing."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-complaints/lodge-a-privacy-complaint-with-us (page title: "Before you lodge a privacy complaint with us")
    - **Page date / version:** Last updated 02 March 2026 [FETCHED]

18. **Fact:** Some privacy complaints about banks may need to go first to AFCA, the approved external dispute resolution (EDR) scheme for banks.
    - **Quote:** AFCA, "who is an approved EDR for banks and financial services."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-complaints/lodge-a-privacy-complaint-with-us
    - **Page date / version:** Last updated 02 March 2026 [FETCHED]

19. **Fact:** Under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, an organisation covered by the Privacy Act must notify affected people and the OAIC when a breach is likely to cause serious harm. It generally has 30 days to assess a suspected breach.
    - **Quote:** "has to tell you if a data breach is likely to cause you serious harm"; "has 30 days to assess"
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/data-breaches/what-is-a-notifiable-data-breach
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

20. **Fact:** A breach notification must include the organisation's contact details, the kinds of information involved, a description of the breach and recommended steps. People should watch for phishing after a breach and use publicly available contact details.
    - **Quote:** The notification includes "Recommended steps you can take"; examples of serious harm include "identity theft, which can affect your finances and credit report" and "financial loss through fraud"
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/data-breaches/what-is-a-notifiable-data-breach
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

## 3. Credit reporting (OAIC)

21. **Fact:** A credit reporting body must give a person their credit report free once every 3 months. A free copy is also available if the person was refused credit in the past 90 days or their information has been corrected.
    - **Quote:** "must give you access to your consumer credit report for free once every 3 months"; "you've been refused credit within the past 90 days"
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/credit-reporting/access-your-credit-report
    - **Page date / version:** Updated 14 April 2026 [FETCHED]

22. **Fact:** The OAIC's current credit-report pages list Equifax (138 332) and Experian (1300 783 684). Different bodies may hold different information. illion is not listed on these updated pages.
    - **Quote:** "Credit reporting bodies may hold different information about you"
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/credit-reporting/access-your-credit-report ; https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/credit-reporting/credit-reporting-terms
    - **Page date / version:** Updated 14 April 2026 [FETCHED]. The OAIC fraud page (fact 24, undated) still names three bodies, including Illion. Confirm the current list before relying on it.

23. **Fact:** To correct an error, a person can contact the credit provider or any credit reporting body. Correction is free, and agreed corrections must be made within 30 days unless the person agrees to longer. If the request is refused, the person must be told in writing and told about EDR or complaint options.
    - **Quote:** "within 30 days or such longer period agreed to by you"; "let you know that you can access an external dispute resolution scheme or make a complaint."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/credit-reporting/correct-your-credit-report
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

24. **Fact:** A person who believes they are, or may be, a victim of fraud can ask credit reporting bodies for a free ban period of 21 days. A ban can be extended any number of times. One body can pass the request to the others.
    - **Quote:** "The ban period will last for 21 days after you first make the request."; "There is no limit on the number of times that a ban period can be extended."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/credit-reporting/fraud-and-your-credit-report
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

25. **Fact:** During a ban, the body cannot use or disclose the credit report without the person's written consent or a legal requirement. It must notify the person at least 5 business days before the ban ends.
    - **Quote:** Notice must be given at least "5 business days" before the end of the ban (paraphrased by the extraction tool); credit providers are told about the ban.
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/credit-reporting/fraud-and-your-credit-report
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

## 4. AUSTRAC (AML/CTF)

All AUSTRAC page fetches timed out, so every fact in this section is [SNIPPET] and must be re-checked.

26. **Fact:** Businesses regulated under AML/CTF laws, including banks, must carry out customer due diligence. This means asking for ID and sometimes questions about a customer's circumstances. The information is protected by the Privacy Act.
    - **Quote:** "When you engage with one of these businesses to use their services, they may ask you for identification or questions about your circumstances. We call this customer due diligence." / "The information you give these businesses is protected under the Privacy Act 1988."
    - **URL:** https://www.austrac.gov.au/general-public/why-you-might-be-asked-id
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]

27. **Fact:** For higher-risk situations, businesses must ask for more information, such as the customer's source of funds or wealth. Alternatives exist for people who cannot provide standard ID.
    - **Quote:** "When a service provider identifies higher risks, they must ask customers for more information, such as proof of source of funds and source of wealth." / "Alternative options may include a referee statement or recently expired identification."
    - **URL:** https://www.austrac.gov.au/general-public/why-you-might-be-asked-id ; https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/customer-due-diligence/initial-customer-due-diligence/identifying-individuals-who-dont-have-standard-id
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]

28. **Fact:** Reporting entities must send AUSTRAC a threshold transaction report (TTR) for each transaction involving A$10,000 or more in physical cash, or the foreign-currency equivalent. Electronic transfers, cheques and card payments do not trigger a TTR.
    - **Quote:** "A TTR must be submitted for each physical currency transaction of $10,000 or more." / "Electronic transfers, cheques and card payments do not trigger TTR requirements"
    - **URL:** https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/reporting-us/threshold-transaction-reports
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]

29. **Fact:** Splitting cash into smaller amounts to avoid reporting ("structuring") is a criminal offence.
    - **Quote:** Search result paraphrase only: structuring to avoid reporting "is a criminal offence"
    - **URL:** https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/reporting-us/threshold-transaction-reports
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET, low confidence]

30. **Fact:** A reporting entity must not tip off a person by disclosing information about a suspicious matter report where that could prejudice an investigation. Asking a customer for identity or source-of-funds information is not tipping off. For customer-facing use, staff should say only that the bank cannot discuss internal reporting.
    - **Quote:** "The new tipping off offence will only prohibit disclosing certain information if it would or could reasonably be expected to prejudice an investigation." / "Asking the customer for more information, including about their identity or what their source of funds is, is not considered tipping off."
    - **URL:** https://austrac.gov.au/about-us/amlctf-reform/current-reporting-entities/tipping ; https://www.austrac.gov.au/business/core-guidance/reporting/suspicious-matter-reports-smrs/tipping
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]. The search summary said the new offence started 31 March 2025; this is not verified.

31. **Fact:** The AML/CTF reforms took effect for existing reporting entities, including banks, on 31 March 2026. Newly regulated sectors (lawyers, accountants, real estate, dealers in precious stones and metals) followed from 1 July 2026. Transitional rules give some businesses until 31 March 2029 to move to the new initial customer due diligence approach.
    - **Quote:** Search result text only, with no clean verbatim quote: updated laws "took effect on 31 March 2026"; 1 July 2026 for newly regulated sectors.
    - **URL:** https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/about-amlctf-reforms/about-reforms ; https://www.austrac.gov.au/about-us/legislation/updates-legislation/amlctf-transitional-rules-2026
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET, low confidence]

## 5. Scams (Scamwatch, Scams Prevention Framework, NASC)

scamwatch.gov.au and accc.gov.au returned HTTP 403 to every fetch. Scamwatch facts are [SNIPPET]. SPF facts come from fetched Treasury and legislation pages where marked.

32. **Fact:** If you've been scammed, contact your bank or card provider immediately and ask them to stop any transactions.
    - **Quote:** "Contact your bank or card provider immediately to report the scam. Ask them to stop any transactions."
    - **URL:** https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]

33. **Fact:** Reports to Scamwatch help the National Anti-Scam Centre (NASC) find the most harmful scams. Scamwatch does not investigate individual reports or reply to them, and a Scamwatch report is not a police report.
    - **Quote:** "Your reports help the National Anti-Scam Centre to identify the scams that are causing the most harm to Australians." (The no-reply and no-investigation points are search-summary paraphrase only.)
    - **URL:** https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam ; https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/about-scamwatch
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]

34. **Fact:** In bank impersonation scams, scammers pose as the bank's fraud team, may fake the bank's caller ID, and push people to move money to a "safe" account. Banks never ask customers to do this.
    - **Quote:** "your bank will never ask you to transfer your money to keep it safe"; scammers "may use technology to make it look like the call is coming from the bank's phone number."
    - **URL:** https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/scam-alert-scammers-impersonating-banks-in-text-messages-phone-calls-and-emails
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]

35. **Fact:** If someone calls claiming to be from your bank, hang up and call the bank back on a number you have found and checked yourself.
    - **Quote:** "contact your bank directly through a phone number that you find and confirm yourself"
    - **URL:** https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/scam-alert-scammers-impersonating-banks-in-text-messages-phone-calls-and-emails
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET]

36. **Fact:** The Scams Prevention Framework Act 2025 (No. 15, 2025) amended the Competition and Consumer Act 2010. Designated businesses must prevent, detect, disrupt, respond to and report scams, and face fines of up to $50 million for failing to.
    - **Quote:** "prevent, detect, disrupt, respond and report scams and attempted scams"; fines "of up to $50 million"
    - **URL:** https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/stephen-jones-2022/media-releases/parliament-passes-world-leading-scams-prevention (13 Feb 2025) ; Act: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2025A00015/asmade/text
    - **Page date / version:** media release 13 February 2025; the Act's register entry shows 20 February 2025 [FETCHED]

37. **Fact:** Banking, telecommunications and some digital platforms were designated on 28 May 2026 as the first regulated sectors. They must be ready for the main obligations on 31 March 2027. As of the date of this research (October 2026), those obligations are not yet in force.
    - **Quote:** sectors must be "ready for 31 March next year," when they will be "required to take stronger action to prevent, detect and disrupt scams."
    - **URL:** https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/daniel-mulino-2025/media-releases/albanese-government-steps-industry-protections-stop ; instrument F2026L00627: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00627/asmade/text
    - **Page date / version:** 28 May 2026 [FETCHED]

38. **Fact:** According to the ACCC, banks providing a regulated service must be AFCA members from 1 September 2026, although most SPF obligations start on 31 March 2027.
    - **Quote:** "although most framework obligations start from 31 March 2027, entities ... who provide a regulated service must be an AFCA member from 1 September 2026"
    - **URL:** https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/scams-prevention-framework
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [SNIPPET; the page returned 403]

39. **Fact:** The Government authorised AFCA to handle external SPF disputes in the banking, telecommunications and digital platform sectors. Consumers must first try to resolve the dispute with the business.
    - **Quote:** AFCA is authorised "to handle external disputes under the Scams Prevention Framework" "across the banking, telecommunications and digital platform sectors"
    - **URL:** https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/daniel-mulino-2025/media-releases/afca-handle-external-disputes-over-scam-losses
    - **Page date / version:** 9 June 2026 [FETCHED]

40. **Fact:** Automatic reimbursement of verified scam losses under $3,000 is only a government position or proposal. It is not an established rule. Draft SPF codes and rules were consulted on from 27 May to 25 June 2026, and the final SPF Rules 2026 (F2026L01140) are dated 31 August 2026 on the register, with operative status not confirmed.
    - **Quote:** The Government "believes" scam victims with verified losses below $3,000 should be automatically reimbursed.
    - **URL:** https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/daniel-mulino-2025/media-releases/albanese-government-steps-industry-protections-stop ; https://consult.treasury.gov.au/c2026-765133 ; https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01140/asmade
    - **Page date / version:** 28 May 2026; consultation closed 25 June 2026 [FETCHED; the F2026L01140 date is SNIPPET]

41. **Fact:** The National Anti-Scam Centre, run by the ACCC, began operating on 1 July 2023. It collects scam reports, including through Scamwatch, and works with banks, telcos and platforms to disrupt scams.
    - **Quote:** search summary only, with no verbatim quote
    - **URL:** https://www.accc.gov.au/national-anti-scam-centre/how-the-national-anti-scam-centre-works

## 7. Consumer Data Right (Open Banking)

44. **Fact:** The CDR lets a person give an ACCC-accredited business ("accredited data recipient") access to their data. It is available in banking, it is opt-in, and consent can be withdrawn at any time.
    - **Quote:** "The Consumer Data Right is an opt-in system."; "You can withdraw your consent at any time."; "It's available in the banking sector."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/consumer-data-right/information-for-consumers/what-is-the-consumer-data-right
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

45. **Fact:** A person can ask for their data to be deleted once it is no longer needed. When withdrawing consent with the provider, they can choose deletion or de-identification.
    - **Quote:** "You can ask for your data to be deleted if it is no longer needed."
    - **URL:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/consumer-data-right/information-for-consumers/what-is-the-consumer-data-right
    - **Page date / version:** not shown [FETCHED]

46. **Fact:** A bank (data holder) must let customers withdraw a sharing authorisation at any time through its consumer dashboard, plus a simple alternative such as phone. It must stop sharing within 2 business days.
    - **Quote:** "Data holders must allow consumers to withdraw their authorisation at any time through the consumer dashboard and must also provide a simple alternative method of communication for this purpose, for example via telephone." / "at most within 2 business days of receiving the communication."
    - **URL:** https://www.cdr.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-07/Compliance-guide-for-data-holders-in-the-banking-and-NBL-sectors-published-11-07-2025.pdf
    - **Page date / version:** published 11 July 2025 [SNIPPET; cdr.gov.au fetches timed out]

47. **Fact:** One joint account holder cannot withdraw sharing authorisations given by another joint holder or a secondary user.
    - **Quote:** "A joint account holder cannot withdraw the authorisations given by other account holders or secondary users."
    - **URL:** https://www.cdr.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-07/Compliance-guide-for-data-holders-in-the-banking-and-NBL-sectors-published-11-07-2025.pdf
    - **Page date / version:** 11 July 2025 [SNIPPET]
