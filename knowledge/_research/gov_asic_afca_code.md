Researched 2026-10-28. Sources are limited to asic.gov.au, moneysmart.gov.au, legislation.gov.au, afca.org.au, ausbanking.org.au and ndh.org.au.

## 1. Financial hardship (National Credit Code s72) and help services

- **Fact:** Under s72 of the National Credit Code, a borrower can tell their credit provider, by phone/verbally or in writing, that they cannot meet their obligations (a "hardship notice").
  - **Quote:** "a debtor may give the credit provider notice, either verbally or in writing, of their inability to meet their obligations"
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/credit-notices-and-offences/faqs-dealing-with-consumers-and-credit
  - **Page date / version:** ASIC INFO 105, "This information sheet was updated in May 2025."

- **Fact:** If the lender has enough information, it has 21 days to tell the borrower its decision on a hardship notice. The 21 days start the day after the lender receives the notice.
  - **Quote:** "The 21-day period begins the day after the hardship notice is received."
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/credit-notices-and-offences/faqs-dealing-with-consumers-and-credit
  - **Page date / version:** updated May 2025

- **Fact:** If the lender needs more information, it must ask for it within 21 days. If the borrower provides it, the lender has 21 days from receiving it to respond. If the borrower does not provide it, the lender has 28 days from the date of its request to respond.
  - **Quote:** "The provider has 21 days to request this information" … "the provider has 28 days from when the information was requested to respond to the hardship notice"
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/credit-notices-and-offences/faqs-dealing-with-consumers-and-credit
  - **Page date / version:** updated May 2025

- **Fact:** If the lender does not agree to change the contract, it must give its reasons and AFCA's contact details.
  - **Quote:** "the credit provider must notify the debtor of:" … "the reasons they do not agree" … "contact details for the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA)"
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/credit-notices-and-offences/faqs-dealing-with-consumers-and-credit
  - **Page date / version:** updated May 2025

- **Fact:** If an agreed hardship change defers or reduces repayments for more than 90 days, the lender must confirm the changes in writing within 30 days of the agreement.
  - **Quote:** "the change defers or reduces the debtor's obligations for a period greater than 90 days" … "The provider must give the debtor this notice no later than 30 days after the date of the agreement"
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/credit-notices-and-offences/faqs-dealing-with-consumers-and-credit
  - **Page date / version:** updated May 2025

- **Fact:** When a customer asks for help, the lender must consider them for hardship assistance.
  - **Quote:** "When you ask for help, your lender must consider you for hardship assistance."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/financial-hardship
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 31 August 2026

- **Fact:** Customers can contact the lender's hardship team by phone, email or chat. Moneysmart says asking early gives more options.
  - **Quote:** "You can reach out to your lender by phone, email or chat." / "The earlier you seek help, the more options you'll have."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/financial-hardship
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 31 August 2026

- **Fact:** Hardship help can be temporary (for example, deferring a payment) or permanent (for example, varying the loan). It can include a payment plan or changed repayments. Moneysmart advises agreeing only to an amount you can afford.
  - **Quote:** Options may be temporary "such as deferring a payment" or permanent "such as varying a loan" (Moneysmart summary of page content)
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/financial-hardship
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 31 August 2026

- **Fact:** A lender that refuses hardship assistance must give reasons, and the customer can complain.
  - **Quote:** "Your lender must give reasons if they refuse your request for hardship assistance."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/financial-hardship
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 31 August 2026

- **Fact:** The National Debt Helpline is 1800 007 007. Phone lines are open weekdays 9:30am–4:30pm and live chat weekdays 9:00am–8:00pm. The service is free and confidential.
  - **Quote:** "Call 1800 007 007" … "Weekdays 9:30am - 4:30pm" … "Chat weekdays 9:00am – 8:00pm" … "It's always free and confidential."
  - **URL:** https://ndh.org.au/ (hours also confirmed at https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/financial-hardship)
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (footer "© Copyright Financial Counselling Australia 2025")

- **Fact:** The National Debt Helpline says a lender has between 21 and 30 days to respond to a hardship request, depending on whether it asks for more information.
  - **Quote:** "between 21 and 30 days to respond"
  - **URL:** https://ndh.org.au/debt-solutions/what-is-financial-hardship-and-what-are-your-rights/
  - **Page date / version:** not shown

- **Fact:** The first time a borrower gives a hardship notice in a three-month period, the lender must stop enforcement or legal action until it responds. This does not apply if the lender already has a court judgment.
  - **Quote:** "must stop further enforcement or legal action until it responds"
  - **URL:** https://ndh.org.au/debt-solutions/what-is-financial-hardship-and-what-are-your-rights/
  - **Page date / version:** not shown

- **Fact:** If a lender refuses a hardship request, the customer can lodge a free dispute with AFCA. If the customer accepts AFCA's decision, it binds the lender.
  - **Quote:** The lender must say it has not agreed, give reasons and explain the right to review. AFCA "can review the decision" (NDH page summary)
  - **URL:** https://ndh.org.au/debt-solutions/what-is-financial-hardship-and-what-are-your-rights/
  - **Page date / version:** not shown

- **Fact (Banking Code):** The Banking Code defines Financial Difficulty broadly. It includes being unable to repay, expecting to be unable to pay upcoming repayments, and having difficulty meeting repayments, for example because of illness, job loss, a pandemic or a natural disaster.
  - **Quote:** "Financial Difficulty means you are unable to repay what you owe, you expect to be unable to pay upcoming repayments, or you are experiencing difficulty meeting your repayment obligations."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (para 168)
  - **Page date / version:** Banking Code of Practice, "Effective from 28 February 2025"

- **Fact (Banking Code):** Examples of hardship help where the customer can recover include short-term interest-only payments, extending the loan term, or temporarily postponing or deferring payments. These may increase the total interest paid.
  - **Quote:** "agreeing to interest only payments for a short period; • extending the term of your Loan to reduce your repayments; or • temporarily postponing or deferring payments."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (Part D1 table)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (Banking Code):** The bank will tell the customer in writing whether it will help with their Financial Difficulty and the reasons for its decision.
  - **Quote:** "184. We will tell you in writing: a) whether we will provide you with help in relation to your Financial Difficulty; and b) the reasons for our decision."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (Banking Code):** Customers can ask the bank to deal with their financial counsellor or representative instead of with them directly. The contact details must be given in writing.
  - **Quote:** "you can tell us to deal with your financial counsellor or representative, rather than dealing with you. To do this, you will need to give us their contact details in writing."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (para 170)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (Banking Code):** The bank will not require a customer to access their superannuation to repay a loan, except when borrowing for an SMSF.
  - **Quote:** "182. We will not require you to access your superannuation to pay any amount you owe us under a Loan (unless you are borrowing for a self-managed superannuation fund)."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (Banking Code):** The bank will not sell a customer's debt while it is actively considering their financial situation under the Code or the National Credit Code hardship provisions, or while they are complying with an agreed arrangement.
  - **Quote:** "190. We will not sell your debt to anyone else if: a) we are actively considering your financial situation"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

## 2. Internal dispute resolution (ASIC RG 271)

- **Fact:** RG 271 sets enforceable IDR standards for credit licensees and AFS licensees, including banks. It applies to complaints received on or after 5 October 2021.
  - **Quote:** "The standards and requirements highlighted in this guide are enforceable." / "This guide comes into effect on 5 October 2021."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (landing page: https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/regulatory-guides/rg-271-internal-dispute-resolution/)
  - **Page date / version:** RG 271, September 2021 (issued 2 September 2021). No later reissue was shown on the landing page.

- **Fact:** A "complaint" is any expression of dissatisfaction where a response is expected. It can be about a product, service, staff or complaint handling.
  - **Quote:** "An expression of dissatisfaction made to or about an organisation—related to its products, services, staff or the handling of a complaint—where a response or resolution is explicitly or implicitly expected or legally required"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (Key terms)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** Firms should acknowledge a complaint within 24 hours (or one business day), or as soon as practicable.
  - **Quote:** "We expect that firms will acknowledge the complaint within 24 hours (or one business day) of receiving it, or as soon as practicable."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (RG 271.51)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** Standard complaints must get an IDR response no later than 30 calendar days after receipt.
  - **Quote:** "A financial firm must provide an IDR response to a complainant no later than 30 calendar days after receiving the complaint."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (RG 271.56, Table 2)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** Credit complaints about default notices, and credit complaints about hardship notices or requests to postpone enforcement, must get a response no later than 21 calendar days after receipt.
  - **Quote:** "Credit-related complaints involving default notices — No later than 21 calendar days after receiving the complaint." / "Credit-related complaints involving hardship notices or requests to postpone enforcement proceedings — No later than 21 calendar days after receiving the complaint."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (Table 2; RG 271.86–271.93)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** For hardship-related complaints with insufficient information, the lender must request information within 21 days. The customer has 21 days to provide it, and the lender then has a further 21 days to respond. If the information is not provided, the lender has 7 days to respond. If agreement is reached, the lender has 30 days to confirm the terms in writing.
  - **Quote:** "the credit provider has a further 21 calendar days to provide an IDR response" … "the credit provider or lessor has 7 calendar days to provide an IDR response" … "has 30 calendar days to confirm the terms or conditions in writing"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (Table 2)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** Superannuation trustee complaints and traditional trustee complaints have a 45-day maximum. This is for context only and is not banking.
  - **Quote:** "No later than 45 calendar days after receiving the complaint."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (Table 2)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** An IDR response must be in writing and state the final outcome, the customer's right to go to AFCA, and AFCA's contact details.
  - **Quote:** "An 'IDR response' is a written communication from a financial firm to the complainant, informing them of: (a) the final outcome … (b) their right to take the complaint to AFCA … (c) the contact details for AFCA."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (RG 271.53)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** If a complaint is rejected or partly rejected, the response must address the issues raised, set out the findings on material facts, and give enough detail for the customer to decide whether to escalate.
  - **Quote:** "the IDR response must clearly set out the reasons for the decision by: (a) identifying and addressing the issues raised in the complaint; (b) setting out the financial firm's findings on material questions of fact …"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (RG 271.54)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact:** A firm can exceed the maximum timeframe only for a particularly complex complaint or circumstances beyond its control. Even then, it must send an "IDR delay notification" before the deadline giving the reasons for the delay, the right to go to AFCA, and AFCA's contact details.
  - **Quote:** "the financial firm must give the complainant an 'IDR delay notification' that informs the complainant about: (a) the reasons for the delay; (b) their right to complain to AFCA if they are dissatisfied; and (c) the contact details for AFCA."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/3olo5aq5/rg271-published-2-september-2021.pdf (RG 271.65–271.66)
  - **Page date / version:** September 2021

- **Fact (Banking Code):** Banks commit to follow RG 271 for complaints. If a complaint is not resolved within 30 days, or 21 days for hardship notices, default notices or requests to postpone enforcement, the bank will explain the delay, give an expected date, provide monthly updates, and tell the customer about AFCA.
  - **Quote:** "If we are unable to resolve your complaint within 30 days (or within 21 days if it involves a hardship notice, default notice or request to postpone enforcement under the National Credit Code), we will: a) tell you the reasons for the delay; b) tell you the date by which you can reasonably expect to hear the outcome … c) give you monthly updates on the progress"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (paras 195–199)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

## 3. AFCA (Australian Financial Complaints Authority)

Every direct fetch to afca.org.au returned HTTP 403. Facts marked UNVERIFIED come from search-index excerpts only.

- **Fact:** AFCA is an independent external dispute resolution scheme for disputes with financial firms. It covers credit and loans, banking deposits and payments, insurance, investments and superannuation.
  - **Quote:** "AFCA is an independent dispute resolution scheme that can help assist you to resolve disputes with financial firms and services."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-to-complain
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 7 August 2026

- **Fact:** AFCA is free for consumers.
  - **Quote:** "The EDR scheme hears complaints for free." (Moneysmart) / AFCA is "a free and independent dispute resolution service" (ASIC)
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-to-complain ; https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/scams/scams-prevention-framework
  - **Page date / version:** Moneysmart last updated 7 August 2026; ASIC page not shown (© 2026)

- **Fact:** AFCA's phone number is 1800 931 678 (9am–5pm, Monday to Friday). The website is www.afca.org.au and online complaints are lodged at my.afca.org.au.
  - **Quote:** "1800 931 678 (9.00am to 5.00pm, Monday to Friday)"
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-to-complain
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 7 August 2026

- **Fact:** Customers should complain to the business first through IDR, then go to AFCA if there is no response in a reasonable time or they are unhappy with the handling or offer.
  - **Quote:** "the first step is always to contact the business to explain the problem and how you'd like it fixed."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-to-complain
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 7 August 2026

- **Fact:** Enforcement action stops while AFCA (EDR) considers a credit dispute. Consumers being sued can still lodge a dispute as long as judgment has not been given.
  - **Quote:** "All enforcement action stops while EDR considers your dispute." / "as long as judgment has not been given"
  - **URL:** https://ndh.org.au/debt-solutions/complaints-and-disputes/
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2025)

- **Fact:** ASIC does not resolve individual complaints or review AFCA decisions.
  - **Quote:** "ASIC has no role in resolving individual complaints or in AFCA's decision making."
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/contact-us/reporting-misconduct-to-asic/what-to-do-if-you-are-dissatisfied-with-a-decision-by-the-australian-financial-complaints-authority
  - **Page date / version:** "updated in March 2024"

- **Fact (UNVERIFIED, search index only):** For most complaints, AFCA's time limit is the earlier of two dates: six years after the consumer first became aware (or should reasonably have become aware) of the loss, or two years after the firm's IDR final response. AFCA may accept late complaints in special circumstances.
  - **Quote (search excerpt):** "within six years after you first became aware, or 'should reasonably have become aware', of the loss, or, if you already complained to the firm … within two years of getting that response" … "whichever of these two options comes first"
  - **URL:** https://www.afca.org.au/media/523/download ; https://www.afca.org.au/what-to-expect/the-process-we-follow (both 403 on direct fetch)
  - **Page date / version:** not verified

- **Fact (UNVERIFIED, search index only):** For complaints lodged on or after 1 January 2024, AFCA's jurisdiction limit is $1,263,000 per claim. Its compensation cap for most direct financial loss is $631,500 per claim. Non-financial loss is capped at $6,300 per claim.
  - **Quote (search excerpt):** "AFCA can award up to $631,500 compensation in most claims of direct financial loss" ; non-financial loss "$6,300 per claim" for complaints lodged on or after 1 January 2024
  - **URL:** https://www.afca.org.au/news/latest-news/afcas-compensation-caps-and-monetary-limits-adjusted ; https://www.afca.org.au/what-to-expect/outcomes-afca-provides (both 403 on direct fetch)
  - **Page date / version:** limits apply to complaints lodged from 1 January 2024. The rules are indexed every three years, so the next adjustment is expected around 1 January 2027. Confirm before relying on these figures after that date.

- **Fact:** From 2026, AFCA is the single external dispute resolution scheme for Scams Prevention Framework complaints against banks, telcos and digital platforms. Covered entities had to join AFCA by 1 September 2026.
  - **Quote:** Entities must be AFCA members by "1 September 2026."
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/scams/scams-prevention-framework
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026)

## 4. ePayments Code: unauthorised transactions and mistaken internet payments

- **Fact:** The ePayments Code is currently a voluntary code administered by ASIC. Most banks, credit unions and building societies subscribe. It covers ATM, EFTPOS, credit card, online and mobile banking, and BPAY. The current version is effective from 2 June 2022. No 2024–2026 revision is listed.
  - **Quote:** "presently a voluntary code of practice"
  - **URL:** https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/epayments-code/
  - **Page date / version:** Code last updated 2 June 2022 (PDF: "ePayments Code Effective from 2 June 2022")

- **Fact:** An account holder is not liable for unauthorised transactions caused by fraud or negligence of bank staff or merchants, by forged, faulty, expired or cancelled cards or passcodes, by transactions before the card or passcode was received, by duplicate debits, or by transactions after the loss or theft was reported.
  - **Quote:** "A holder is not liable for loss arising from an unauthorised transaction if the cause of the loss is any of the following: (a) fraud or negligence by a subscriber's employee or agent …"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 10.1)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** The holder is not liable where it is clear the user did not contribute to the loss.
  - **Quote:** "A holder is not liable for loss arising from an unauthorised transaction where it is clear that a user has not contributed to the loss."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 10.3)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** The holder is liable only if the bank proves, on the balance of probability, that the user contributed to the loss through fraud or a passcode security breach. A passcode breach alone is not enough. Even then, liability is capped by daily or periodic limits and the account balance.
  - **Quote:** "A breach of the passcode security requirements in itself is not sufficient to make a consumer liable for loss from an unauthorised transaction."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 11.2 note)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** A user who unreasonably delays reporting a lost or stolen device, or a compromised passcode, can be liable for losses that occur during the delay, subject to transaction limits.
  - **Quote:** "Where a subscriber can prove, on the balance of probability, that a user contributed to losses … by unreasonably delaying reporting the misuse, loss or theft of a device …"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 11.5)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** Where a passcode was required and no other liability rule applies, the holder's liability is limited to the lowest of $150 (or less, if the bank sets less), the available balance, or the actual loss at the time of reporting.
  - **Quote:** "the holder is liable for the least of: (a) $150, or a lower figure determined by the subscriber"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 11.7)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** Moneysmart advises reporting suspected unauthorised transactions to the bank as soon as possible and keeping a reference number.
  - **Quote:** "If you think something is wrong, contact your bank as soon as possible."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/unauthorised-and-mistaken-transactions
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 17 September 2026

- **Fact:** Under the ePayments Code, the process for a mistaken payment depends on when it is reported: within 10 business days, between 10 business days and 7 months, or after 7 months.
  - **Quote:** "different rules apply if you make the report within 10 business days, after 10 business days, or after seven months."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/unauthorised-and-mistaken-transactions
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 17 September 2026

- **Fact:** For a mistaken payment reported within 10 business days with enough funds in the recipient's account, the receiving bank must return the funds within 5 business days if practicable, and no later than 10 business days. The recipient's consent is not needed.
  - **Quote:** "the receiving ADI must return the funds to the sending ADI, within 5 business days of receiving the request from the sending ADI, if practicable, or such longer period as is reasonably necessary, up to a maximum of 10 business days."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 30.1–30.2)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** For a mistaken payment reported between 10 business days and 7 months, the receiving bank investigates within 10 business days. If satisfied, it freezes the funds for 10 more business days. It returns them unless the recipient proves entitlement within that time.
  - **Quote:** "The receiving ADI must complete its investigation into the reported mistaken payment within 10 business days of receiving the request." … "prevent the unintended recipient from withdrawing the funds for 10 further business days"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 31.2–31.4)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** For a mistaken payment reported after 7 months, funds are returned only if the unintended recipient consents.
  - **Quote:** "If the receiving ADI is satisfied that a mistaken internet payment has occurred, it must seek the consent of the unintended recipient to return the funds to the user."
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 32.2)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

- **Fact:** Banks must show an on-screen warning before a BSB and account number payment that funds may go to the wrong account and may not be recoverable. This warning requirement does not apply to PayID payments.
  - **Quote:** "if the user enters or selects an incorrect BSB number or identifier, funds may be sent to the wrong account and it may not be possible to recover funds from an unintended recipient"
  - **URL:** https://download.asic.gov.au/media/lloeicwb/epayments-code-published-02-june-2022.pdf (cl 27.1–27.2)
  - **Page date / version:** June 2022

## 5. Banking Code of Practice (2025 version)

- **Fact:** The current Code is the 2025 Banking Code of Practice. ASIC approved it on 27 June 2024 and it is effective from 28 February 2025. It replaces the version dated 5 October 2021. A 2026 Banking Code Review is underway, with submissions due 11 December 2026.
  - **Quote:** "The 2025 Banking Code of Practice was approved by ASIC on 27 June 2024." / "Effective from 28 February 2025"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/banking-code/ ; https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf
  - **Page date / version:** 2025 Code, effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (inclusive banking):** Banks will take reasonable measures to make services accessible. This covers older customers, people with disability, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander customers, people with limited English, and people of diverse sexual orientations, gender identities and sex characteristics. Where practicable, banks will arrange free interpreters (including Auslan), the National Relay Service, and accessible information.
  - **Quote:** "organising or referring you to external support free of charge, including: a) interpreter services (for example via qualified interpreters, including Auslan); b) National Relay Services"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (paras 45–46)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (First Nations):** Banks will help Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander customers meet ID requirements when they lack standard documents, following AUSTRAC guidance.
  - **Quote:** "help you meet any identification requirements if you do not have access to standard identification documents, by following AUSTRAC's guidance"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (para 48)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (vulnerability):** Banks commit to taking extra care with customers experiencing vulnerability. Listed risk factors include age, disability, mental health, elder abuse, family or domestic violence, financial abuse, Financial Difficulty, and language barriers. Customers are encouraged to tell the bank about their circumstances.
  - **Quote:** "We are committed to taking extra care with customers who are experiencing vulnerability." … "g) family and/or domestic violence; h) financial abuse"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (para 52)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (low income):** Customers who tell the bank they have a Commonwealth Seniors Health Card, Health Care Card or Pensioner Concession Card will be given information about basic, low-fee or no-fee accounts.
  - **Quote:** "a) a Commonwealth Seniors Health Card; b) a Health Care Card; or c) a Pensioner Concession Card."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (para 57)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (guarantors):** Before accepting a guarantee, the bank will take reasonable steps to hold a meeting with the guarantor (in person, video or phone) without the borrower present. This does not apply if the guarantor has received independent legal advice, and some exceptions apply.
  - **Quote:** "Before we accept your guarantee, we will take reasonable steps to ensure that a meeting is held with you either in person or via video conference, phone, or some other means to discuss you being a Guarantor."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (paras 109–111)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (guarantors):** The bank will not accept a guarantee until the third day after the guarantor receives the required information, unless exceptions apply (for example, the guarantor confirms they had independent legal advice).
  - **Quote:** "We will not accept a guarantee from you until the third day after you have been given the information provided at paragraphs 103 to 105."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (paras 112–113)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (guarantors):** Within 14 days, the bank will send guarantors copies of formal demands or default notices, and notices of borrower hardship changes or continuing default of more than two months.
  - **Quote:** "We will send to you the following information, about a borrower's deteriorating financial position as it relates to the Loan you guarantee, within 14 Days of the relevant event"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (para 115)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (deceased estates):** Once verified documents are received, the bank will act on a valid Deceased Estate Representative instruction within 14 business days.
  - **Quote:** "we will act on a valid Deceased Estate Representative instruction concerning a deceased's accounts within 14 business days of receiving that instruction."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (paras 138, 143)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (deceased estates):** For sole-name accounts, the bank will promptly stop fees for services that can no longer be provided and refund any such fees charged since the death. Before probate, representatives can still request account information, pay debts, and release funds for funeral expenses (with invoices).
  - **Quote:** "promptly identify and stop charging any fees that are for Banking Services that can no longer be provided" … "c) instruction to release funds to pay or reimburse permissible expenses such as funeral expenses"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (paras 136, 139)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact (small business):** Under the 2025 Code, a business is a "Small Business" if it (or its group) had annual turnover under $10 million in the previous financial year, has fewer than 100 FTE employees, and has less than $5 million total debt to all credit providers.
  - **Quote:** "(a) had an annual turnover of less than $10 million in the previous financial year; and (b) has fewer than 100 full-time equivalent employees; and (c) has less than $5 million total debt to all credit providers"
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (Part E definitions)
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

- **Fact:** Customers can complain about the bank's compliance with the Code free of charge, through IDR or AFCA. The independent Banking Code Compliance Committee monitors compliance.
  - **Quote:** "You can make a Complaint about our Banking Services or our compliance with the Code."
  - **URL:** https://www.ausbanking.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Banking-Code-of-Practice-28.02.25.pdf (para 195); https://www.ausbanking.org.au/banking-code/
  - **Page date / version:** Effective 28 February 2025

## 6. Moneysmart: scams, responsible lending, credit reports, comparison rate

- **Fact (scams):** Anyone scammed should contact their bank or card provider immediately, report the scam to Scamwatch, change passwords, and contact IDCARE on 1800 595 160 for a free response plan.
  - **Quote:** "Contact your bank or card provider immediately to report the scam." / "Report the scam to Scamwatch to help stop the scam and protect others." / IDCARE "1800 595 160"
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 13 August 2026

- **Fact (scams):** Be wary of "recovery" offers to get scammed money back for a fee or percentage. These are a common follow-up scam.
  - **Quote:** anyone who "claims they can recover your losses for a percentage of the recovered losses"
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 13 August 2026

- **Fact (scams):** If unhappy with the bank's response to a scam, the customer can make a formal IDR complaint and then go to AFCA.
  - **Quote:** "you can make a complaint with the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA)"
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 13 August 2026

- **Fact (scams, regulatory change):** The Scams Prevention Framework imposes enforceable obligations on banks to prevent, detect, disrupt, report and respond to scams. Most obligations start on 31 March 2027.
  - **Quote:** "imposes enforceable obligations to combat scams" / "Most SPF obligations will commence on 31 March 2027."
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/scams/scams-prevention-framework
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026)

- **Fact (responsible lending):** Credit licensees must not enter into a credit contract that is unsuitable for the consumer. They must make reasonable inquiries, verify the consumer's financial situation, and assess that the contract is "not unsuitable". The consumer can request a written copy of the assessment.
  - **Quote:** "reasonable inquiries about a consumer's financial situation, and their requirements and objectives" / "reasonable steps to verify a consumer's financial situation"
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/responsible-lending
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026)

- **Fact (responsible lending):** Lenders cannot lend if they think the borrower won't be able to make the repayments.
  - **Quote:** "They can't lend you money if they think that you won't be able to make the repayments."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/loans/loan-rejection
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 24 August 2026

- **Fact (credit reports):** Consumers can get a free copy of their credit report every 3 months. They can get one more often if they were recently rejected for credit.
  - **Quote:** "you have a right to get a copy of your credit report for free every 3 months." / "every three months, or more frequently if you provide evidence that you have been rejected for credit recently."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/credit-scores-and-credit-reports ; https://moneysmart.gov.au/loans/loan-rejection
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 9 September 2026 / 24 August 2026

- **Fact (credit reports):** Moneysmart names Experian and Equifax as the two main credit reporting bodies. Defaults stay on a report for five years (seven for a clearout). Repayment history covers the last two years. Fixing errors is free.
  - **Quote:** "two main credit reporting bodies" … defaults "five years" or "seven years in the case of a clearout" … "This is a free service."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/credit-scores-and-credit-reports
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 9 September 2026

- **Fact (comparison rate):** A comparison rate combines the interest rate and most fees and charges into a single percentage, to show the true cost of a loan.
  - **Quote:** "It includes the interest rate, and most fees and charges relating to a loan, reduced to a single percentage figure."
  - **URL:** https://moneysmart.gov.au/glossary/comparison-rate
  - **Page date / version:** Last updated 24 August 2019

## 7. Unclaimed money (ASIC)

- **Fact:** Money from old bank accounts that a bank cannot return to the owner after seven years of inactivity is held by ASIC if it is more than $500. Claims for amounts under $500 are made with the bank.
  - **Quote:** "Any unclaimed amount more than $500 sits with ASIC." / "Any claims related to old bank accounts under $500 should be made with the bank."
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/australians-reminded-to-check-for-lost-money-as-27-billion-sits-unclaimed
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (cites data March 2025–March 2026)

- **Fact:** The inactivity period has been seven years since 31 December 2015. It was three years from 2012 to 2015. Some account types are exempt, and customers should ask their bank.
  - **Quote:** "from 31 December 2015 – the period of inactivity is seven years" / "Exemptions for particular account types apply - speak with your bank."
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/unclaimed-money/changes-to-the-commonwealth-unclaimed-money-laws
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026)

- **Fact:** People search for unclaimed money with the free Moneysmart search tool. Searching and claiming are free, and there is no deadline to claim.
  - **Quote:** "To find unclaimed money, do a search on Moneysmart." / "The search and claims process is completely free" / "There is no deadline to make a claim"
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/unclaimed-money ; https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/australians-reminded-to-check-for-lost-money-as-27-billion-sits-unclaimed
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026)

- **Fact:** To claim unclaimed bank money, the consumer contacts the bank (ADI) first. The bank verifies entitlement and claims from ASIC on their behalf. Interest has been paid on claims since 1 July 2013.
  - **Quote:** "they should contact the ADI to begin their claim." / "From 1 July 2013 when unclaimed money is paid to a claimant, the Commonwealth of Australia will also pay interest."
  - **URL:** https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/unclaimed-money/how-authorised-deposit-taking-institutions-lodge-unclaimed-money-claims
  - **Page date / version:** not shown (© 2026)
