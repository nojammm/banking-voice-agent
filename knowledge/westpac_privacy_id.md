Westpac's Privacy Statement (effective August 2026) says Westpac collects personal and contact details; government identification such as Medicare number, passport details and driver's licence number, and copies of ID documents; call recordings when customers contact call centres or branches; transaction records; and device and app data such as device IDs and IP addresses. Sensitive information collected can include citizenship, residency status and biometric data, used to verify identity and authorise transactions. Westpac collects personal information to provide products and services, verify identity, prevent and respond to fraud and crime, and comply with legal and regulatory requirements in Australia and overseas.

Westpac may share information overseas, including with service providers likely located in New Zealand, the US, Canada, India, Japan, the Philippines, the UK, Malaysia, Brazil, South Africa and the European Economic Area, and with Westpac Group companies in Germany, Hong Kong, Singapore, New Zealand, the UK, the United States, Fiji and Papua New Guinea.

Under Westpac's Privacy Statement (effective August 2026), customers can ask to access or correct their personal information by calling 132 032 (+61 2 9155 7700 from overseas), visiting a branch, using Westpac's secure online feedback form, or writing to Reply Paid 5265, Sydney NSW 2001, which is also the address for Westpac's Privacy Officer. Westpac verifies identity before giving access or making changes. There is no fee to request or make corrections; in some circumstances Westpac may charge a reasonable amount for providing access. If Westpac refuses access or correction, it gives written reasons and explains how to complain.

A customer who is not happy with Westpac's response can go to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) on 1800 931 678, or complain under the Privacy Act to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) on 1300 363 992, GPO Box 5288, Sydney NSW 2001.

Westpac's Credit Reporting Policy, part of its Privacy Statement (effective August 2026), says Westpac uses two credit reporting bodies: Equifax (13 83 32, equifax.com.au) and Experian (1300 783 684, experian.com.au). Westpac is required to tell credit reporting bodies if a customer fails to meet consumer credit payment obligations or enters a financial hardship arrangement, and may disclose a serious credit infringement such as obtaining credit by fraud. Customer consent is not required for a credit information request, and such requests may affect a credit score.

Under comprehensive credit reporting, Westpac reports monthly repayment history information (RHI). Westpac also reports financial hardship information (FHI), a letter listed each month on a credit report: "A" for a temporary hardship arrangement or "V" for a permanent variation of the credit contract. Westpac says customers can request a free copy of their credit report from each credit reporting body at least every 3 months. If a customer notices an error that Westpac reported on their credit report, Westpac asks them to contact Westpac.

Westpac asks customers to confirm their details as part of its Know Your Customer (KYC) requirements, even if nothing has changed, because all Australian banks must meet Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing (AML/CTF) requirements. The fastest way to confirm details is in the Westpac App or Online Banking. Westpac says it will never ask customers to reply to an email to confirm or update their details, and will never send an email or SMS with a link that goes directly to its sign-in page.

If a customer does not confirm their details when Westpac asks, Westpac may need to restrict access to their banking until they do, which could also affect accounts held jointly or accounts they are a signatory to. Westpac says it will endeavour to give at least 14 days' notice before restrictions apply. A customer unsure whether an identity verification request is genuine can hang up and call Westpac on 1300 720 314 (+61 2 9155 7570 from overseas) and choose the team that contacted them: Know Your Customer, Customer Verification or Foreign Tax.

Westpac says Open Banking lets customers share their banking data with parties they trust, but only Accredited Data Recipients (ADRs), which must be accredited by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) and comply with privacy and security requirements. A personal customer starts at the ADR's consent portal and is then connected to Westpac to authorise the share using their Westpac Customer Number and a One Time Password through Westpac Protect SMS. For security, Westpac says customers cannot use their Online Banking password for data sharing. Westpac will only share a customer's data with an ADR if the customer gives permission, unless required by law, and an ADR must only use the data for the purpose the customer requested.

## Stopping open banking data sharing with Westpac's data sharing dashboard

Westpac customers can see all active consents, and past consents that expired or were revoked or cancelled, in the data sharing dashboard in Westpac Online Banking. A customer can withdraw permission to share data on any account at any time using the dashboard; Westpac then stops sharing and sends a notice as soon as practicable. A consent can be a one-time share or last up to a maximum of 12 months, after which a new consent is needed. Any joint account holder can disable data sharing on a joint account through the dashboard, and re-enabling it needs all joint holders to approve.
